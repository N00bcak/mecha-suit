# Communication Evidence

This note records the basis for the Communication rules in
`templates/global-AGENTS.template.md`. It is maintenance context, not a
substitute for those instructions.

## Cognitive Load

- Chandler and Sweller's experiments support integrating related material
  rather than forcing a reader to combine scattered sources:
  <https://doi.org/10.1207/s1532690xci0804_2>
- Kalyuga's expertise-reversal review shows that guidance useful to novices can
  become redundant or harmful for experts:
  <https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-007-9054-3>
- Research on signaling supports headings and cues that direct attention, but
  not decorative formatting or the removal of necessary relationships:
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5576760/>

Design implication: remove presentation work, not reasoning. Keep related
evidence and qualifications together, and adapt scaffolding to the conversation.

## Conversation

- Grice treats useful conversation as a balance of adequate evidence,
  relevance, clarity, and the right amount of information:
  <https://lawandlogic.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/grice1975logic-and-conversation.pdf>
- Clark and Brennan describe conversation as collaborative grounding. The goal
  is to reduce combined effort across presenting, understanding, confirming,
  and repairing:
  <https://web.stanford.edu/~clark/1990s/Clark%2C%20H.H.%20_%20Brennan%2C%20S.E.%20_Grounding%20in%20communication_%201991.pdf>

Design implication: acknowledge and restore shared state when it prevents
repair, but do not repeat a checkpoint mechanically.

## Plain And Technical Language

- The National Archives recommends audience-aware writing, major points first,
  short focused paragraphs, active voice, and useful headings and lists:
  <https://www.archives.gov/open/plain-writing/10-principles.html>
- W3C cognitive-accessibility guidance favors familiar words, short blocks,
  separated instructions, and unambiguous content:
  <https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/objectives/o3-clear-content/>
- The Australian Government Style Manual permits specialist terms when the
  audience uses them and recommends explaining unfamiliar ones:
  <https://www.stylemanual.gov.au/writing-and-designing-content/clear-language-and-writing-style/plain-language-and-word-choice>
- Google and Microsoft technical-writing guidance favors coherent paragraphs,
  direct openings, conversational tone, and non-blaming help:
  <https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/one/paragraphs>
  <https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/style/writing-style>

Design implication: plain expression does not mean imprecise or childish
expression. Prefer the user's words and preserve exact artifact terminology.

## Orwell

George Orwell's *Politics and the English Language* warns that ready-made
phrases, inflated diction, stale metaphors, passive concealment, and vague
abstraction can obscure both meaning and thought:
<https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/>

Design implication: decide the meaning before choosing the words. Prefer
concrete, direct language, remove dispensable words, and break any style rule
that would otherwise damage accuracy or clarity.
