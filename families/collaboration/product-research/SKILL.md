# Product Research

## Purpose

Resolve a bounded product or domain question with evidence that can change a
product decision, domain model, or validation plan.

## Do Not Use

- Do not own technical feasibility, APIs, source code, or software standards;
  use `technical-research`.
- Do not make the user's product decision or turn findings into a product brief;
  use `product-co-design` for that judgment.
- Do not claim primary user evidence from desk research.
- Do not spawn, request, or rely on subagents.

## Needs

- The decision and bounded research question.
- Intended users or domain, relevant geography and time horizon.
- Available sources, prior evidence, constraints, and required confidence.

## Returns

- Evidence-backed findings with citations or reproducible observations.
- Explicit inferences, limitations, confidence, and contrary evidence.
- Implications for the decision without silently making it.
- A durable research artifact when requested or lifecycle mode is active.

## Done Conditions

- The question is answered to the confidence the decision requires.
- Decision-relevant claims trace to appropriate sources or observations.
- User evidence, market evidence, inference, and recommendation remain distinct.
- The next product, domain, or validation decision is explicit.

## Workflow

1. Read `../../../shared/rubrics/research-integrity.md`.
2. State the decision, question, scope, and evidence threshold.
3. Inspect supplied evidence and discover existing project research before
   seeking more.
4. Prefer direct user evidence, first-party data, authoritative domain sources,
   and primary materials. Record time-sensitive scope and access dates.
5. Seek evidence that could disconfirm the leading interpretation.
6. Synthesize findings, inference, limitations, confidence, and decision
   implications.

Do not substitute competitor marketing for user evidence or popularity for
fitness. When direct research with people is required, return a concrete study,
interview, observation, or experiment design; do not invent participants or
results.

## Progress

Progress is a verified claim, rejected assumption, narrower uncertainty, or
decision-changing finding. When stakes justify it, test one credible contrary
source or explanation before returning confidence and the next check.
