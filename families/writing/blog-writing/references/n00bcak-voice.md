# N00bcak Blog Voice

This profile is distilled from public posts across `docs/blog/` and
`docs/writeups/`, spanning technical explanations, research reports,
competition recaps, challenge solutions, and criticism. It describes a range,
not a character to perform.

## Stable Tendencies

- Begin near the actual motivation: a puzzle, result, irritation, surprising
  observation, or concrete project.
- Write in first person when the author participated, tested, designed, failed,
  or changed their mind.
- Move freely between technical precision and casual commentary without
  translating established domain terminology into bland language.
- Explain difficult material by showing the reasoning path, intermediate
  observations, and why an otherwise plausible approach is strange or fails.
- Make judgments directly. Qualify them when evidence is incomplete, not as a
  ritual display of neutrality.
- Use headings and lists to support long technical paths; use prose when making
  an argument.
- Let sentence and paragraph length vary with the pace of the explanation.

## Technical And Research Posts

Use a comparatively measured register.

- State the question, method, or result early.
- Include a TL;DR when the post is long or experiment-heavy.
- Link code, papers, prior work, and relevant implementations.
- Distinguish preliminary results from durable conclusions.
- Explain formal machinery in ordinary language before or alongside notation.
- Retain enthusiasm or frustration, but let evidence carry the claim.

These posts can contain jokes and asides, but technical understanding is the
reader payoff.

## Competition Recaps And Personal Technical Posts

Allow more visible personality.

- Establish the event, result, collaborators, and unusual circumstances.
- Admit delays, mistakes, reliance on tools, or questionable implementation
  choices when they are part of the story.
- Use self-deprecation and exaggeration selectively around real events.
- Transition quickly from scene-setting into what was built, tried, or solved.

Do not invent emotional reactions or interpersonal details to make a recap
livelier.

## CTF Writeups

Optimize for reproducible understanding.

- Preserve the challenge statement, artifacts, constraints, and flag details
  supplied by the author.
- Move through reconnaissance, background, key insight, implementation, and
  verification in the order that best explains the solve.
- Explain why a detail looks suspicious and how that changes the next step.
- Keep domain-specific slang where it is natural and intelligible from context.
- Include code and results that materially establish the solution.
- Separate intended difficulty, observed difficulty, and retrospective design
  commentary when relevant.

## Polemical Posts

Use the sharpest register only when the post is actually making a contentious
argument.

- Open with the concrete frustration or disputed claim.
- Represent opposing claims specifically before challenging them.
- Mix blunt conclusions with technical or experiential support.
- Use rhetorical questions, comic asides, repetition, and heightened emphasis
  as pressure points rather than continuous decoration.
- End with a position, consequence, or actionable proposal instead of a generic
  summary.

Strong language does not excuse weak evidence or invented consensus.

## Surface Habits

Available, never mandatory:

- Parenthetical asides and ellipses.
- Strategic capitalization, bold emphasis, emoticons, and playful headings.
- Singaporean, CTF, ML, and internet-native vocabulary.
- Abrupt short sentences after longer explanations.
- Rhetorical questions and direct address.
- Self-aware comments about lateness, difficulty, clankers, or questionable
  choices.

Use these only when the underlying situation earns them. Do not insert one of
each, repeat signature phrases mechanically, or copy memorable wording from a
source post.

## Avoid Caricature

- Do not make every introduction angry, chaotic, or self-deprecating.
- Do not force slang into formal research exposition.
- Do not create fake autobiographical details, beefs, jokes, or opinions.
- Do not preserve an old quirk when a nearby current post shows that the site
  convention has changed.
- Do not smooth away mixed register, uneven cadence, or deliberate repetition
  merely because a generic style guide disfavors them.

Choose the quietest profile that still sounds recognizably appropriate for the
material.
