# Long-Form Prose Revision Patterns

Use this reference for substantial revision, humanization, or a requested
style audit. These are contextual editing signals, not evidence about who or
what wrote a text.

## Calibrate First

Establish the genre, audience, purpose, and existing voice before changing
anything. Academic caution, report structure, technical terminology, polished
grammar, passive constructions, and formal transitions may be correct.

Look for repeated or interacting habits. One word, sentence shape, em dash, or
rhetorical question is rarely a useful diagnosis by itself.

## Meaning And Evidence

Avoid:

- Replacing a precise claim with a smoother but broader claim.
- Removing uncertainty that the evidence requires.
- Adding examples, motives, reactions, or quotations that were not supplied.
- Turning a source-backed statement into vague consensus.

Prefer:

- Preserve the exact claim and qualification, then improve its expression.
- Name the source, actor, mechanism, date, or result when the inputs support it.
- Flag an evidence gap instead of writing around it.

## Abstraction And Inflation

Revise passages that repeatedly announce significance without showing it:
generic claims about importance, transformation, broader implications, or
timeless lessons often hide a missing argument.

Avoid: “This development marks a pivotal shift in the evolving landscape.”

Prefer: state what changed, for whom, and with what observable consequence.

Keep a broad claim when the piece subsequently earns it with evidence.

## Filler And Meta-Prose

Cut throat-clearing that explains the act of writing rather than advancing the
piece: generic previews, recap conclusions, ceremonial transitions, and
phrases that announce that something deserves attention.

Avoid: “It is important to note that the second trial failed.”

Prefer: “The second trial failed.”

Keep navigation when a long report genuinely needs it.

## Attribution And Confidence

Vague authorities and padded hedges weaken prose when specificity is available.
The opposite error—deleting every hedge—creates false certainty.

Avoid: “Experts generally believe the method may offer meaningful benefits.”

Prefer: identify the study or observation and state the supported conclusion.

Preserve “may,” “suggests,” or similar language when it represents real
uncertainty rather than politeness.

## Rhythm And Structure

Review clusters of:

- Similar sentence lengths or openings.
- Paragraphs that each follow claim, explanation, summary.
- Repeated three-item lists or paired contrasts.
- Mechanical transition words at every boundary.
- Fragments, parentheticals, questions, or dashes inserted on a schedule.

Vary structure because the argument changes pace, not to satisfy a pattern
quota. A deliberately repetitive passage may be the strongest part of a piece.

## Stock Personality

Do not manufacture humanity through compulsory contractions, slang,
self-deprecation, first-person anecdotes, rhetorical questions, jokes, or
opinions. Match supplied samples and retain genuine quirks, including mixed
registers or unusual punctuation, when they serve the author.

Avoid replacing one generic AI voice with another generic “casual human” voice.

## Formatting

Use headings, lists, tables, quotations, and emphasis when they clarify real
structure. Remove decorative formatting that breaks an argument into fragments
or repeats the same information in multiple forms.

Protect citations, footnotes, links, equations, code, frontmatter, and quoted
material unless the task specifically includes them.

## Final Pass

1. Compare every factual claim and qualification with the inputs.
2. Read for continuity of argument, not just sentence polish.
3. Check that edits preserve the author's vocabulary level and stance.
4. Remove changes made solely to obey a heuristic.
5. Leave strong original passages alone.
