# Research Integrity

Use this rubric for product, domain, software, and architecture research.

## Claim Types

Label material claims by their role when confusion is possible:

- **Sourced:** stated by an identified primary or authoritative source.
- **Observed:** directly inspected, measured, reproduced, or calculated.
- **Inferred:** concluded from sourced or observed evidence.
- **Assumed:** provisionally accepted without confirming evidence.
- **Proposed:** a candidate interpretation or action.
- **Decided:** a user-owned or otherwise authorized choice.
- **Unknown:** unresolved and capable of changing the conclusion.

Do not present an inference, assumption, or proposal as a fact or decision.

## Source Discipline

- Prefer the source that owns the claim: official documentation, source code,
  specifications, standards, first-party data, original research, or direct
  user evidence.
- Use secondary sources to discover primary material or represent a viewpoint,
  not to replace an available owning source.
- Cite each decision-relevant claim close to the claim.
- Record access or observation date when facts can change.
- Quote only when exact wording matters; otherwise paraphrase faithfully.
- Redact secrets, credentials, private identifiers, and unnecessary personal
  data from notes and captured output.

## Reasoning Discipline

- State the bounded question before gathering evidence.
- Seek evidence capable of changing the answer, including one plausible
  contrary explanation when stakes justify it.
- Separate evidence collection from recommendation.
- State confidence and the evidence or missing fact that controls it.
- Stop when the decision can be made responsibly, further work would repeat,
  or a specific unavailable source blocks progress.

## Return

Return the question, findings, citations or reproducible observations,
inferences, limitations, confidence, and the decision or next discriminating
action the evidence supports.
